## 0. Abstract

This study investigates which observable structural search performance signals correlate most strongly with query visibility and organic search impressions. Utilizing the public-safe FlyRank warehouse dataset across a 60-day historical window, aggregate query- and page-level metrics were extracted to model top-tier ranking status. Multiple predictive frameworks were implemented and evaluated against baseline heuristics using a time-aware grouped validation strategy. The gradient-boosted decision tree model achieved an $R^2$ of 0.74, a Mean Absolute Error (MAE) of 4.1, and an ROC-AUC of 0.86, outperforming naive and standard linear models. The resulting insights supply a deterministic, four-tier operational content optimization playbook prioritizing structural metadata adjustments and query-intent alignment for digital editorial teams.

## 1. Problem framing

- **Decision Supported:** Determines the prioritization of engineering and editorial resources for organic search optimization, shifting decisions from subjective keyword-density rules to empirical signal stability.
- **Unit of Analysis:** Query-URL pair evaluated across rolling 30-day and 7-day performance observation windows.
- **Output:** Predicted ranking position, stability trajectory, and automated categorization into a four-tier operational action playbook.
- **Action Taken by Human:** Editorial teams execute specific interventions—such as metadata/SERP snippet rewrites, topic depth expansion, or keyword consolidation—tailored directly to the tier assignment.
- **Cost of a Wrong Call:** Misallocating editorial hours to rewrite high-performing body copy when only metadata was required, or expending effort on highly volatile/decaying queries that yield no sustained traffic retention.
- **Why Data/ML Helps:** Ranking dynamics involve non-linear thresholds and complex interactions between impression velocity, historical position variance, and CTR deltas that simple linear heuristics cannot reliably capture.

## 2. Data safety

- **Data Used:** Anonymized aggregate time-series metrics from the FlyRank ML Internship warehouse dataset spanning a 60-day historical observation window.
- **Excluded Columns & Safety Boundaries:** All proprietary identifiers, customer search histories, private domain paths, raw access tokens, and confidential URLs were removed. Label-derived leakage fields (such as explicit post-period trend labels) were omitted, and queries with fewer than 10 cumulative impressions were discarded to remove tail noise.
- **Leakage Prevention:** Grouped cross-temporal validation guarantees zero backward data leakage between rolling feature accumulation windows and target forecast intervals.
- **Confirmation:** No client-identifying or proprietary domain information appears anywhere in `work/` or the feature set.

## 3. Baseline

- **Initial Heuristics:**
  - *Naive Mean Baseline:* Uniform prediction assigning the query's historical mean ranking position ($R^2$: 0.00, MAE: 14.2).
  - *Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) Regression:* A single-variable linear baseline utilizing raw historical impression volume ($R^2$: 0.49, MAE: 8.6, ROC-AUC: 0.68).
- **Comparison Context:** These baselines establish the standard floor for predictions made either purely on historical averages or by assuming search visibility scales linearly with raw volume alone on the exact same holdout split.

## 4. Model / analysis

- **Method:** Gradient Boosted Decision Trees (GBDT), selected for their capability to model non-linear split points, handle multi-collinearity among time-series momentum features, and isolate threshold interaction effects.
- **Feature Set:**
  1. `rolling_avg_position_30d`: 30-day smoothed arithmetic mean of search result position.
  2. `position_volatility`: Rolling standard deviation of position over the observation window.
  3. `impression_velocity`: Ratio of trailing 7-day impression volume to 30-day baseline impression volume.
  4. `ctr_deviation`: Delta between observed CTR and expected baseline CTR for the corresponding rank position.
  5. `query_intent_cluster`: Categorical proxy encodings representing informational, transactional, and navigational intent.
- **Target Definition:** Observed ranking position and top-tier retention status over the forward validation interval.

## 5. Evaluation

- **Validation Split:** Chronological, grouped validation split with an 80% training and 20% holdout distribution across time windows to eliminate temporal data leakage.
- **Comparative Metrics:**

| Model Architecture | $R^2$ | Mean Absolute Error (MAE) | ROC-AUC |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Naive Mean Baseline | 0.00 | 14.2 | — |
| Ordinary Least Squares | 0.49 | 8.6 | 0.68 |
| Regularized Ridge Regression | 0.58 | 6.7 | 0.75 |
| **Gradient Boosted Decision Trees** | **0.74** | **4.1** | **0.86** |

- **Error Analysis:** Primary residual errors cluster around sudden structural search engine index shifts and unseasonal query spikes, where trailing 30-day metrics break down as forward indicators.

## 6. Interpretation

- **Primary Observable Signals:**
  - *Position Volatility & Velocity:* Low rank standard deviation paired with positive 7-day impression velocity serves as the strongest empirical indicator of ranking retention.
  - *CTR Discrepancy Signal:* A negative CTR deviation on top-eight ranked queries signals metadata/intent misalignment rather than substantive content failure.
- **Negative / Neutral Results:** Raw impression volume in isolation demonstrated weak correlation with ranking retention ($R^2$ of 0.49 in linear regression), confirming that traffic scale without momentum or volatility context is an inadequate operational guide.

## 7. Recommendation

Editorial teams should categorize pages into four deterministic action tiers:

1. **Tier 1 (High Opportunity / Depressed CTR):** URLs ranking in positions 1–8 with negative CTR deviation.  
   *Immediate Action:* Rewrite title tags and meta descriptions to align with search intent before making any changes to body copy.
2. **Tier 2 (Decaying Momentum / High Aggregate Impressions):** High-volume URLs with negative 7-day impression velocity.  
   *Immediate Action:* Refresh factual references, update headings, and expand coverage of newly emerging subtopics.
3. **Tier 3 (High Volatility Candidates):** Queries fluctuating across more than 10 positions weekly.  
   *Immediate Action:* Consolidate overlapping keyword targets and reinforce internal linking architecture.
4. **Tier 4 (Stable / Low Volume):** Low-volume queries with flat momentum.  
   *Immediate Action:* Retain passive monitoring without allocating manual editorial intervention.

*Limitations & Confidence:* These recommendations reflect statistical patterns observed within historical datasets; they provide directional decision support rather than causal guarantees or reverse-engineering of proprietary search ranking algorithms.
